1. Ease is not medical care

This is the most important section here. Please read it properly.

Ease is a wellness and tracking tool. It is not a medical device, and it does not provide medical advice, diagnosis or treatment.

• Nothing in Ease is a diagnosis. In particular, Ease does not diagnose PCOS, endometriosis, thyroid conditions or anything else — and no app can. Only a qualified clinician can.
• The nutrition, exercise, sleep and stress guidance in Ease is general educational information. It is not a prescription, not a treatment plan, and not tailored to your medical history, your medication or your diagnoses.
• The patterns Ease shows you are observations about what you recorded. They are a starting point for a conversation with a doctor, not a finding to act on by yourself.
• Always consult a qualified healthcare professional before changing your diet, exercise, supplements or medication, and about any symptom that worries you. Never disregard or delay professional medical advice because of something you read in Ease.

Ease is not contraception
Ease must not be used to prevent or to achieve pregnancy.

The cycle predictions in Ease are estimates calculated from the dates you log. They are frequently imprecise, and they are least reliable in exactly the circumstances many of our users have — irregular cycles, PCOS, perimenopause, recently stopping hormonal contraception, illness, or significant stress.

Ease does not detect ovulation. Where the app shows an "ovulatory" phase, that is an estimate derived from your logged dates, not a measurement. Please speak to a healthcare professional about contraception and about trying to conceive.

In an emergency, contact your doctor or emergency services immediately. Ease is not for emergencies and cannot help you in one.

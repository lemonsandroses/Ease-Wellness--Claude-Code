3. What we collect

Everything below is given to us by you. Ease has no advertising SDK, no analytics SDK and no tracking pixel. It does not collect your location, contacts, photos, microphone or camera. It contains no in-app purchases and takes no payment information of any kind.

Account information

  • Email address
  • Name, if you give one during onboarding
  • Date of birth, which is required to confirm you are 18 or over
  • A randomly generated account identifier

If you sign in with Apple or Google, we receive only the identity token they issue — your email address (or Apple’s private relay address, if you chose to hide it) and, where provided, your name. We never receive your Apple or Google password.

Health information

This is special-category personal data under GDPR Article 9 and sensitive personal data under the DPDP Act, and we treat it accordingly. It includes what you choose to log:

  • Menstrual bleeding days and flow level
  • Symptoms you record and their severity
  • Your typical cycle length, period length and last period start date
  • Energy, mood, stress and sleep entries
  • Movement and exercise entries
  • Meals and their nutritional breakdown
  • Free-text notes you write on any day
  • Health focus areas and baseline symptoms chosen during onboarding

Calendar information

Only if you connect Google Calendar. We read upcoming events so the app can show them alongside your cycle, and we store the event title, date, time, location and notes. We never write to, modify or delete anything in your Google Calendar. Disconnecting stops all further access. See section 6 for the limits we accept on this data.

5. Where it is stored, and how it is protected

Your data is held in a Postgres database operated by Supabase, hosted in the AWS Asia Pacific (Mumbai) region, ap-south-1. Personal data of users in India therefore stays within India.

  • Row-level security on every table. Database policy, not application code, enforces that a signed-in account can only ever read or write its own rows. This has been verified by adversarial testing: one account could not read, modify or delete another’s data under any tested condition.
  • Encrypted in transit (TLS) and at rest.
  • The key embedded in the app grants no access without a valid session, and a session only ever unlocks that one account’s data.

A copy is also kept on your device so the app works offline.

Reproductive health data is sensitive in a way that goes beyond regulation. Specific commitments:

9. Your rights

Under the GDPR, the DPDP Act and other applicable laws you may:

  • Access the data we hold about you
  • Correct anything inaccurate — most of it is editable in the app
  • Delete your account and all associated data — Settings → Delete my account
  • Withdraw consent at any time, by deleting your account
  • Port your data to another service
  • Object to processing, or ask us to restrict it
  • Complain to a supervisory authority (in the EU/UK, your local data protection authority; in India, the Data Protection Board)

Most of these you can exercise yourself inside the app. For anything else, email easetrackerapp@gmail.com and we will respond within 30 days.

10. Age

Ease is available only to people aged 18 and over. Date of birth is required when you create an account, and an account cannot be created with a date of birth under 18. We do not knowingly hold data about anyone younger. If you believe someone under 18 has an account, contact easetrackerapp@gmail.com and we will delete it.

We set the threshold at 18 deliberately. Many people begin menstruating well before then, and we are sorry to turn younger users away — but processing health data about minors carries obligations under the DPDP Act and GDPR Article 8 that we would rather meet properly than meet badly.

11. International transfers

Your data is stored in India (AWS ap-south-1). If you use Ease from outside India, your data is transferred to and processed in India. Where data leaves the EEA or UK we rely on Standard Contractual Clauses.
